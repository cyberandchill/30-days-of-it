# Portfolio maintenance contract

Follow `docs/WORKFLOW.md` and `docs/EVIDENCE.md`. Public repository: do not disclose credentials, tenants, private hosts, tokens, or sensitive image details. Preserve authentic evidence.

## CyberAndChill technical portfolio presentation
- The homepage headline is **CyberAndChill | Technical Portfolio**. Present CyberAndChill as a candidate and potential technical service provider. No 30-day challenge branding or counters belong in this portfolio; challenge content is a separate marketing project. Do not show a completed-project counter or unfinished-day list. Preserve the concise visual gallery.
- Keep the public portfolio focused only on the candidate's projects and demonstrated results. Do not link free how-to material teaching others to construct portfolios; that instruction is reserved for a separate paid course.
- Homepage cards must fit a fast hiring-manager scan: project title, technology subtitle, simple architecture diagram, and one two-line summary. No verified-result block, metrics grid, verification badge or long-form evidence on cards; these belong on the clicked project pages. Keep the cards visually compact.
- Homepage is a clean gallery of clickable architecture cards, not long-form screenshots or logs. Never reference Ship 30 for 30 or another challenge.
- Each completed project's README must be succinct: what was built, why, visual, prominent **Follow the manual GUI walkthrough** link, key result, expandable technical details.
- Each published project needs `GUIDE.md`: self-contained GUI-first numbered steps stating exact navigation, clicks, values/options to enter, prerequisites and expected result. Use the fewest steps needed to reproduce the outcome; do not narrate every agent action. Be accurate about version-dependent paths.
- Put 4–10 **authentic, public-reviewed, redacted screenshots** inline beside the steps they illustrate; keep the full screenshot collection available separately. Never fabricate UI/screenshots or pretend an illustration is actual execution evidence.
- Explain differences between recommended manual practice and historically implemented automation. Link related projects for prerequisites without repeating tutorials.
- Public pages must not describe the owner's AI agent, agent instructions, orchestration tools, hypervisor, remote voice workflow, or how automated builds and publishing are executed; those are reserved for a separate paid product. Preserve ordinary named technologies needed to understand the lab itself.
- Do not link private workflow archives from public-facing README files. Review screenshots, captions, SVG text, guides, metadata and linked files before publication.
- Scripts, tests, hashes, detailed build logs and implementation workflows are not part of the public portfolio tutorial. Keep private materials outside the public repository.
- Update the renderer's templates and source data in tandem so future renders preserve the presentation.

Read relevant day metadata and source records before editing. Preserve existing projects and NOTES.md. Never infer verified outcomes from plans. Do not rerun or alter lab infrastructure just to maintain the portfolio. Review outputs, evidence, secret scans and diff before committing. Push non-forced and verify the published files.

- Cards use wide landscape SVGs (900×530 viewBox) at up to 760px width. Keep large, readable typography, two or three clearly connected architecture nodes, consistent padding, one concise outcome sentence, and a single explore link. No results block or small-print diagrams.
